## <font color="#388bfd">Objective</font>

You will research and present a cryptocurrency or blockchain technology of your choice. Your presentation should answer the key questions: **Who, What, How, Where, When, and Why.**

This is the module's consolidation: every concept you have learned — ledgers, hash links, consensus, wallets, tokens, smart contracts — becomes a lens for examining a real project. A strong presentation shows the class something *you* found in the code and tokenomics that they could not get from a headline.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Choosing a topic</font>

Choose **one cryptocurrency or blockchain technology in the top 100** on [coinmarketcap.com](https://coinmarketcap.com/). First come, first served within a class period — claim early. As you browse candidates, useful sorting questions:

- Is it a **coin** (its own blockchain, like Bitcoin) or a **token** (a contract on another chain, like the ERC20 you deployed)?
- Is it Proof of Work, Proof of Stake, or something else?
- Is the code open source? (If you cannot find the code, finding the "How" will be hard — pick something else.)

---

## <font color="#388bfd">What your presentation must cover</font>

- **Who:** Background on the creator(s) and origin story.
- **What:** What the technology is and how it works.
- **How:** A **code snippet key to its functionality and novelty** (for open-source projects). Describe its **tokenomics** — how the token is minted, distributed, and allocated — its blockchain values, technical features, token distribution, and what makes it unique.
- **Where & When:** Where and when it was developed and implemented.
- **Why:** The purpose behind its creation and its significance.
- **Additional:** Comparisons to similar technologies, and a brief look at future prospects or technical challenges.

> **Tip:**
> The "How" is where this class differs from a marketing pitch. You have read `chainparams.cpp` and `ERC20.vy` — do the same here: find the file where your project's key behavior lives and show the lines. Tokenomics questions you can now answer from code: who can mint, is supply capped, who got the initial allocation?

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Presentation guidelines</font>

- **Duration:** 4–6 minutes
- Use slides with **minimal text** and clear visuals
- Engage your audience by speaking confidently — **do not simply read your slides**
- Show respect to your peers during their presentations

Full grading grid and submission details are in the [assignment](ASSIGNMENT.md).
