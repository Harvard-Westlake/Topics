# Assignment - Widget Presentations

**Lesson:** [Widget Presentations](README.md)  
**Due:** End of class (in-class work, nothing to submit)

---

## Why Today

You spent the last class reviewing a classmate's widget: testing it, writing up problems, and getting it fixed. Today you show the class that widget, and what your review did to it.

---

## Your Presentation (under 3 minutes)

Present **your partner's widget**, the one you reviewed, using the speaker notes you prepared in the peer review assignment:

| Part | Length | What you show |
|---|---|---|
| **Walkthrough** | 1 minute | The live widget from arriving to the payoff, the way a first-time visitor would use it. |
| **What you tested** | about 30 seconds | The checks you ran, and the one way you tried hardest to break it. |
| **What you found** | about 30 seconds | The most important problem from your review, live or as a screenshot. |
| **What got fixed** | about 30 seconds | How it was fixed, or why it is still open, and your final status. |

Have the live widget and your review open in two browser tabs before your turn.

---

## Order

1. Partners present back to back: first one reviewer, then the other. Each widget gets shown by the person who reviewed it.
2. Your teacher calls the order. The next pair gets ready while the current one presents.
3. The widget's author may add one sentence at the end, and only one.

---

## While Others Present

For every presentation, write down:

- One idea from the widget you would use in your own.
- One problem from the review you want to check for in your own widget.

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] **Walkthrough in one minute:** you showed the live widget from arriving to the payoff.
- [ ] **Review explained:** you covered what you tested, what you found, and what got fixed.
- [ ] **Under three minutes:** your whole presentation fit the time.
- [ ] **Notes taken:** one idea and one problem written down for every other presentation.

---

## Submission

Nothing to submit. This is all in-class work.
