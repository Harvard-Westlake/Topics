# Assignment - Collaborative Peer Review

**Lesson:** [Collaborative Peer Review](README.md)  
**Due:** Before the start of next class

---

## What to Build

A finished review of your assigned partner's Learning Widget, and a presentation of it ready for next class.

Your teacher posts the partner list. Each of you reviews the other's widget.

### Part 1: The Review

| Result | What it means |
|---|---|
| **Access both ways** | You have Write access on your partner's repository, and they have it on yours. |
| **A posted review** | A review on their pull request with sections for **Functional**, **Correctness**, **Errors**, **UX**, **UI vs /admin**, and **Suggestions**, with steps to reproduce and a screenshot for every problem. |
| **Problems driven to a fix** | Every problem was fixed (by you or the author) or formally requested as a change, and you re-tested after each round. |
| **An honest final status** | You approved only once nothing you know of is still broken. Otherwise the PR stays at **Request changes**. |

### Part 2: Presentation Prep

Next class you present **your partner's widget**, the one you reviewed. Build it straight from your review:

| Part | Length | What you show |
|---|---|---|
| **Walkthrough** | 1 minute | The live widget, from arriving to the payoff, the way a first-time visitor would use it. |
| **What you tested** | about 30 seconds | The checks you ran, and the one way you tried hardest to break it. |
| **What you found** | about 30 seconds | The most important problem from your review, shown live or as a screenshot. |
| **What got fixed** | about 30 seconds | How it was fixed, or why it is still open, and your final status. |

Write a short speaker note for each part, and have the live widget and your review open in two browser tabs.

**Verify:** run through the whole presentation out loud once with a timer. It fits in under 3 minutes, and the walkthrough alone takes about 1.

## Grading Rubric

| Criterion (weight) | Full credit | Partial or no credit |
|---|---|---|
| **Functional behavior** (25) | Every core feature works as stated. | Key features missing, broken, or unclear. |
| **Correctness** (20) | What it teaches matches real, checked sources, including edge cases. | Wrong or unchecked content. |
| **Errors and stability** (15) | No Console errors on load or during use. | Errors, flaky behavior, or unhandled failures. |
| **UX quality** (10) | Engaging, quick to respond, clear feedback and empty states, works on a phone. | Clunky, slow, or confusing. No phone support unless your teacher excused it. |
| **UI clarity** (10) | Labels and paths are obvious without explanation. | Hard to discover or misleading. |
| **UI match with `/admin`** (10) | Looks and behaves like the `/admin` page. | Noticeably different. |
| **Review write-up and accountability** (10) | Every section, steps to reproduce, every problem called out, no approval with known bugs. | Vague notes, missed bugs, or approval with known problems. |

---

## Success Criteria

- [ ] **Access granted both ways:** you have Write access on your partner's repository, and they have it on yours.
- [ ] **Every check run:** you tested function, correctness, errors, UX, and the match with `/admin`.
- [ ] **Review posted:** your review on their PR has all six sections, with steps to reproduce.
- [ ] **Problems resolved:** fixes were committed or formally requested until the PR met the bar.
- [ ] **Approval is honest:** you approved only with no known bugs left.
- [ ] **Presentation ready:** speaker notes for all four parts, timed under 3 minutes.
