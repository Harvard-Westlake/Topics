# Team Design and Pitch
*<font color="#8b949e">Present your design, absorb your team's best ideas, and build one cohesive plan</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Reinforce</font>

</div>

---

Today you present your design to your team and answer questions about it. Your teammates take notes, then the team discusses the features and behaviors they liked — and integrates the best ideas from everyone into a **single, cohesive design**, committed to the repository owned by the original designer. The team then prepares a presentation representing the entire design for next class.

**Objectives:**

- Develop an understanding of collaborative design processes
- Enhance presentation and communication skills
- Foster team collaboration and the integration of diverse ideas into a cohesive design

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Today's plan</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">1. Introduction (10 minutes)</font>

As a class, we revisit design thinking and collaborative design, and set the day's objective: present individual designs, discuss as a team, and integrate ideas into one cohesive design.

### <font color="#79c0ff">2. Individual presentations (40 minutes)</font>

- Each student presents their design to their team — focus strictly on presenting and answering questions **about the design**
- Other team members take notes on aspects they find innovative or effective

### <font color="#79c0ff">3. Team discussion (15 minutes)</font>

After all presentations, the team discusses the designs it found useful, including:

- Alterations to current behaviors
- New behaviors
- Feature additions

Steer away from:

- Worrying about how to program something
- Technical details about how an idea would be solved

### <font color="#79c0ff">4. Design integration (30 minutes)</font>

- Work together to combine the best features from each design into a single, cohesive plan
- Update the Git repository **owned by the original designer** with the integrated design
- Use clear commit messages — version control discipline matters in collaborative projects

### <font color="#79c0ff">5. Preparation for the final presentation (20 minutes)</font>

- Start preparing a presentation that represents the entire team design
- Be creative in how you showcase the work — slides, live walkthroughs, whatever sells it
- Allocate roles within the team for the final presentation

### <font color="#79c0ff">6. Homework</font>

Teams finalize their presentations.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Next class: final presentation</font>

Each team presents its cohesive design to the class, followed by constructive feedback and discussion from peers and instructors.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Evaluation</font>

- Individual presentation skills — clarity, content, and engagement with questions
- The integrated design — creativity, functionality, and how well it incorporates various ideas
- Teamwork and collaboration during the design integration phase
- The Git repository — proper use of version control and clear documentation of changes

We close with a brief reflection where students share what they learned from the collaborative process. This lesson blends technical skills (design, version control) with the soft skills — presentation and teamwork — that real software teams live on.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">☑️ Check for Understanding</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Name the two things a presenter is allowed to do during individual presentations (present, and answer questions about the design).
- [ ] List the three kinds of ideas a team discussion should surface (altered behaviors, new behaviors, feature additions).
- [ ] Say whose repository receives the team's integrated design.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Take notes during a presentation that identify *why* a feature is effective, not just that you liked it.
- [ ] Explain what the team must steer away from during discussion, and why.
- [ ] Write a commit message for an integration commit that makes clear which ideas were merged in.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Combine two overlapping features from different designs into one coherent behavior, rather than keeping both.
- [ ] Explain what makes an integrated design "cohesive" instead of a list of everyone's favorite ideas.
- [ ] Divide presentation roles so every team member presents the part they know best.
