# Learning to Design
*<font color="#8b949e">Describe what a game does — in plain language, before any code talk</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Learning</font>

</div>

---

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to articulate and document the **behaviors**, **functions**, and **relationships** within a game, using non-technical language — and tell the difference between a design description and a technical specification.

> **Warning:**
> Today you are **not** to use ChatGPT or any other AI tool. The entire point of this lesson is to practice articulating ideas in your own words. AI-written design documents defeat the exercise.

**Learning objectives:**

- Analyze and understand the gameplay mechanics of a video game
- Create a non-technical game design document detailing the behaviors, functions, and relationships within the game
- Distinguish technical descriptions of game mechanics from behavioral design

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Part 1 — Play, observe, and write</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">1. Explain a game's behavior (10 minutes)</font>

We introduce a game — or your partner introduces theirs — with a brief background, the basic rules, and the objectives. Pay attention to the game's *elements* while playing; you will need them for the writeup.

### <font color="#79c0ff">2. Playtime (10 minutes)</font>

Play your partner's game, individually or in pairs. Take notes on the game's mechanics, characters, behaviors, and any relationships you observe between game elements.

### <font color="#79c0ff">3. Game design writeup (30 minutes)</font>

Write a game design document describing **your partner's** game. It must include these sections:

| Section | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| **Game Concept** | What is the main idea of your partner's game? |
| **Main Characters** | Who are the players or main elements in the game? |
| **Game Environment** | What does the game world look like? |
| **Behaviors** | How do the characters or elements in the game behave? |
| **Functions** | What can the player do in the game? |
| **Relationships** | How do different elements of the game interact with each other? |

> **Tip:**
> Write every sentence so that someone who has never programmed could picture the game. The moment you mention a variable, a loop, or a function name, you have left design and entered specification.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Part 2 — Design vs technical specification</font>

Now that you have written a design, learn to spot what is *not* design.

### <font color="#79c0ff">1. Review the examples</font>

Read both worked examples in [Design vs Tech Spec](../Docs/design-vs-tech-spec.md):

- **Game design vs technical specification — Pac-Man**
- **App design vs technical specification — ATM Application**

### <font color="#79c0ff">2. Trade and evaluate</font>

1. **Trade design documents** with your paired classmate.
2. **Evaluate your partner's document** — identify which parts are game design elements and which fall under technical specifications:
   - **Create a list** categorizing the sections and points from your partner's document into "Game Design" and "Technical Specifications." If your partner wrote nothing you would consider technical, instead write technical specifications for **5 methods found in your own game's code**.
   - **Provide feedback** — a brief paragraph on how well your partner separated design from technical specification, and any places where the two were confused or blended.
   - **Find missing behaviors** — identify at least one design behavior or feature they missed.

### <font color="#79c0ff">3. Reflect</font>

Talk with your partner briefly about what you learned. What was hard about distinguishing design from technical specification? How might this change how you approach designing your own game or app?

## <font color="#388bfd">☑️ Check for Understanding</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Name the six sections of a game design writeup.
- [ ] Describe one behavior from a game you played today in fully non-technical language.
- [ ] State the quick test that separates a design sentence from a specification sentence.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Explain the difference between a game's *behaviors* and its *functions*.
- [ ] Take one sentence from your writeup and decide — with a reason — whether it is design or specification.
- [ ] Find a design behavior your partner's document missed.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Rewrite a technical sentence like "the ghost's state variable switches to FRIGHTENED" as pure design language.
- [ ] Explain a *relationship* between two game elements and why it matters to the player's experience.
- [ ] Explain why this lesson banned AI tools, in terms of what skill the lesson builds.
