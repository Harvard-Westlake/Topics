# Design Review
*<font color="#8b949e">Critique a partner's design without sliding into implementation</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Reinforce</font>

</div>

---

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Review an idea **without** falling into describing implementation or technical details
- Keep all conversation on ideas and their value

Here is the core skill: separating ***designing for users*** from ***implementing that design in a computer***. Collaboration, critical thinking, and clear communication are needed for both — but today, stay focused entirely on the ideas and behaviors that make a design valuable **for the user**.

> **Tip:**
> Before you write a single comment, skim [Design Communication](../Docs/design-communication.md). The subject of every sentence you write should be the design — never the designer.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Class activity</font>

Take **10 minutes of reading time** to read your partner's design. Then watch their pitch video.

### <font color="#79c0ff">0. Become a collaborator</font>

Have your partner add you as a collaborator on their repository, and accept the invite.

> **Warning:**
> When you add documentation or review comments to their repository, you must work in a **branch other than `main`**. Their `main` branch is their design — your review lives alongside it until they accept it.

### <font color="#79c0ff">1. Design review</font>

Read the design as a skeptical user, and write comments directly into the document (in your branch):

- Where is the design **unclear**? If you cannot picture what happens, say exactly where you got lost
- Where is the design **incomplete**? Which behaviors are named but never described?
- Which parts drifted into **implementation** — technical language that belongs in a spec, not a design?

### <font color="#79c0ff">2. Honor Code and law violations</font>

Check the design for anything that would violate the Honor Code or the law: copied intellectual property, features that exclude or target groups of students, privacy violations, or anything that conflicts with the [California non-discrimination clause](https://tinyurl.com/canondescriminationclause) requirements from the Design Challenge. Flag anything questionable in your review.

### <font color="#79c0ff">3. Constructive ideas (glaze only if deserved)</font>

Add ideas that would genuinely make the design better for users — new behaviors, sharper value, smoother experience. Praise is allowed **only when it is earned and specific**. "This is great!" with no reason is empty; "the daily streak mechanic gives a concrete reason to return, and it compounds with the leaderboard" is a real review.

### <font color="#79c0ff">4. Commit and submit a pull request</font>

Commit your review comments in your branch and open a **pull request** so your partner can see, discuss, and integrate your feedback.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">In-person comments</font>

After the written review, talk it through face to face. Walk your partner through your biggest points, and let them ask questions. Practice the same discipline out loud: critique the design, never the designer.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Homework</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Integrate the review comments</font>

Work through the review you received and integrate the feedback into your own design.

> **Note:**
> The **end** design should still be a comprehensive explanation of your great idea and how it works in detail. A review is raw material — your design document absorbs the good ideas and stays coherent.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Reformat the design to Markdown</font>

Your design must live in your repository as clean Markdown — headings, lists, and tables — not as an exported blob of text. From here on, every design in this unit is versioned, diffable Markdown.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Skill Building</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Can you explain why review work happens in a branch other than `main`?
- [ ] Can you name the four steps of the in-class review (collaborator, design review, honor-code check, constructive ideas)?
- [ ] Can you give one example of a review comment whose subject is the design, not the designer?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Can you spot a sentence in a design that has drifted into implementation, and explain why it belongs in a spec?
- [ ] Can you write a piece of specific, earned praise — and explain why unspecific praise is worthless in a review?
- [ ] Can you name two kinds of Honor Code or legal problems a design could contain?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Can you integrate a critical review into your design so the final document reads as one coherent idea, not a patchwork?
- [ ] Can you run the full workflow — branch, comment, commit, pull request — without notes?
- [ ] Can you explain the difference between reviewing an idea's value and reviewing its feasibility, and why today is only about the former?
