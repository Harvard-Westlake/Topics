# Communicating Design
*<font color="#8b949e">Brainstorm the best idea ever — and sell it in terms of user value alone</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Learning</font>

</div>

---

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

- Communicate designs focused on **user value, behavior, and experience**
- Deliberately *exclude* implementation, feasibility, and technical requirements from your thinking — focus on the best idea ever, without limits
- Recognize the importance of aesthetics and functionality without delving into technical implementation

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Introduction</font>

Design is the creative process of envisioning and planning how something will look and function *before it comes into existence*. Whether it is a building, a garment, a product, or a game, the design is the blueprint that guides its creation. Today we explore the fundamental aspects of design — purpose, aesthetics, and functionality — all in terms of the **user**. We are careful never to discuss technical implementation.

> **Warning:**
> Every part of your idea must be curated *intentionally* by *you*. When asked about any part of it, you must be able to explain at length why you chose it, and what its meaning and purpose are. We are not building AI slop — we are building **your** idea.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The design brainstorm process</font>

A brainstorm works in two directions:

### <font color="#79c0ff">Forward brainstorming</font>

Generate as many ideas as possible without judging any of them. Quantity first — write down the obvious, the absurd, and the impossible. Feasibility is explicitly *not* a filter today; the best idea ever is allowed to stretch what seems possible.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Reverse brainstorming</font>

Flip the question: instead of "what would make this product great?", ask **"what would make this product terrible?"** List everything that would make it frustrating, boring, or unusable — then invert each item into a design requirement. "Ads interrupt every action" becomes "the experience is never interrupted." Reverse brainstorming is often faster at finding what users actually care about, because we are all experts at what we hate.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Tips for ideas</font>

- Start from a problem you or your friends genuinely have — value is easier to explain when it is real
- Steal the *feeling* of products you love, never the product itself
- Say your idea out loud in one sentence; if the sentence needs a second sentence, sharpen it
- If you are stuck, design for one specific person you know, then generalize

## <font color="#388bfd">Activity — design or technical specification?</font>

For each item below, decide: does it belong in the **design**, or in the **technical specification**?

- Utility of the game / app
- User experience
- Visual aesthetics
- Performance and reliability
- Accessibility
- Scalability
- Protecting user data
- Updatability
- Engagement

Discuss disagreements — several of these are genuinely arguable, and the argument is the lesson. Review the worked examples in [Design vs Tech Spec](../Docs/design-vs-tech-spec.md) if you need calibration.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Design your app / program / game</font>

Now design your own product — anything that lives in a computer. Do not worry about technology or implementation; we just want a *good product*. Your design will be pitched to the class, so it needs to communicate:

1. **The problem and the audience** — who is this for, and why is it engaging or useful?
2. **The concept** — main features, sketches, and the user flow (for apps) or progression (for games)
3. **The behaviors** — the key mechanisms that make it engaging
4. **The look** — aesthetics chosen on purpose, not by default

The full deliverables, point breakdown, and grading rubric are in the [assignment](ASSIGNMENT.md).

> **Note:**
> Everyone in the class will see your presentation. The objective is to **sell** your idea. It needs to stand out and be the best.

## <font color="#388bfd">☑️ Check for Understanding</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Define design as a process, in one sentence, without mentioning computers.
- [ ] Explain what reverse brainstorming is and give one example of inverting a "terrible" item.
- [ ] Say what three things a design communicates about a product (value, behavior, experience).

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Sort "scalability" and "user experience" into design or technical specification, and defend your choices.
- [ ] Explain why implementation and feasibility are deliberately excluded from today's brainstorm.
- [ ] Explain, for any single element of your design, why you chose it and what purpose it serves.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Pitch your idea in one sentence that names the user and the value, with zero technical words.
- [ ] Take a competitor product and articulate the *feeling* it delivers, separate from its features.
- [ ] Argue why "each part curated intentionally by you" produces a better product than an AI-generated design.
