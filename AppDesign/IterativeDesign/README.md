# Iterative Design
*<font color="#8b949e">Define "better" — then make an existing design better, commit by commit</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Reinforce</font>

</div>

---

This lab guides you through **iterating on an existing design concept**. You will dive into design thinking, breathe additional life into a selected concept, and continue collaborating through GitHub. Your job: elevate a chosen design's interactivity and engagement — and get comfortable using GitHub for collaborative design projects.

**Expected outcomes:**

- Define what "better" design means to you
- Critically analyze a design and creatively enhance it
- Experience GitHub: version control and collaboration

> **Tip:**
> This is about **evolution** — not just adding, but enhancing. Take what exists and turn it into something you would want to engage with, then articulate that transformation to your peers.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The task</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">1. Your definition of "better"</font>

- Define "better" in your own words, focused on interactive design
- Align your definition with the chosen design's potential — better *for this design*, not better in the abstract

### <font color="#79c0ff">2. Improvement blueprint</font>

- Detail your design improvement plan
- Explain each enhancement — **why** it uplifts the design and **how**

### <font color="#79c0ff">3. GitHub action</font>

- **Clone** the chosen repository (and become a collaborator)
- **Create a branch** to work in
- **Commit at least three times.** Additions should be rich in detail
- **Prepare a presentation** — next class is show and tell for your ideas

**Working instructions:**

- Understand the current design first — **read** and examine all existing information
- Improve the design, and be explicit about what improvement means to you
- Commit with purpose — each commit should be meaningful
- Be ready to discuss your creative choices with your group next class

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The presentation rule</font>

You will present your improvements next class. You must have something to show — even if it is your written content — but you are **not to look at it during the presentation**.

> **Warning:**
> Not reading off your notes is worth **half of your presentation points**. Pictures are fine to look at; prose is not. Know your own ideas well enough to speak them.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Skill Building</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Can you state your personal definition of "better" for interactive design in two sentences?
- [ ] Can you clone a repository, create a branch, and push a commit to it?
- [ ] Can you name the three GitHub actions this lesson requires (clone as collaborator, branch, three commits)?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Can you explain, for one of your enhancements, both why it improves the design and how it works for the user?
- [ ] Can you explain what makes a commit "meaningful" versus padding to hit a count?
- [ ] Can you present one of your improvements out loud without looking at your notes?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Can you distinguish *adding to* a design from *enhancing* it, with an example of each from your own work?
- [ ] Can you critique your own definition of "better" — what does it prioritize, and what does it ignore?
- [ ] Can you trace how your three commits tell the story of your improvement plan in order?
