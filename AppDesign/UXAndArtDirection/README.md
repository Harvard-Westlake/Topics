# UX and Art Direction
*<font color="#8b949e">Two tracks — design how it flows, or design how it feels</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Learning</font>

</div>

---

The challenge today is not to do great work on the project — it is to make sure the work done is **communicated effectively**. Without proper communication, the work is meaningless.

**Learning objectives:**

1. **Understand the roles** — the distinct responsibilities of UX Designers and Art Directors in app/game development
2. **Develop specific skills** — practical skills in your chosen track, UX Design or Art Direction
3. **Apply knowledge creatively** — conceptualize and plan elements of an app or game project

> **Note:**
> These are just two of many roles on a real team. Browse the full [Game Dev Jobs](../Docs/game-dev-jobs.md) catalog to see where these fit among producers, programmers, level designers, sound designers, and more.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The two roles</font>

| | UX Design | Art Direction |
|---|---|---|
| **Owns** | User experience, interface design, the user journey | Visual style, theme, the creation of art assets |
| **Asks** | Can the user get where they want, without friction or confusion? | Does everything the user sees belong to one coherent world? |
| **Produces** | Flowcharts, wireframes, user-testing plans | Mood boards, palettes, sprites, sketches, audio direction |
| **Judged by** | Clarity and functionality | Aesthetic appeal and thematic consistency |

Choose a track. You will work in that group for the hands-on session, then join a **mixed team** for the integrated assignment.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Hands-on — UX Design track</font>

- Create a **simplified UX flowchart** for the app/game — every screen the user can reach and every path between screens
- Sketch a **basic wireframe** for a key screen or interaction — boxes and labels, not art
- **Plan user testing** — decide how and when you will incorporate user testing and feedback

> **Tip:**
> A wireframe answers "what is on this screen and where," never "what color is it." If you are picking colors, you have wandered into the other track.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Hands-on — Art Direction track</font>

- Generate a **color palette** for the project: [Coolors — the fast color palette generator](https://coolors.co/)
- Try building **animated pixel characters**: [Piskel — free online sprite editor](https://www.piskelapp.com/)
- Develop a **mood board** showcasing the visual theme and style
- Create **initial sketches or digital art** aligned with the chosen theme
- Consider **audio and sound effects** — sound is part of the direction
- Emphasize **visual storytelling and consistency** — every asset should feel like it comes from the same world

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Integrated assignment — mixed teams</font>

Form mixed teams containing both UX Design and Art Direction students. Together, develop a concept for an app or game that incorporates both disciplines.

**Requirements (by your path):**

1. A **UX flowchart and a wireframe** for a key part of the app/game
2. A **mood board and several art assets** matching the project's theme

Details, evaluation criteria, and submission are in the [assignment](ASSIGNMENT.md).

**Overall takeaway:** UX Design and Art Direction are different jobs that only succeed together. By the end of this lesson you should understand both fields and have practical experience integrating them into one cohesive project.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Skill Building</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Can you state what UX Design owns and what Art Direction owns, in one sentence each?
- [ ] Can you name the two artifacts the UX track produces and the two main artifacts the art track produces?
- [ ] Can you explain what a mood board is for?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Can you draw a UX flowchart for a three-screen app without describing any visuals?
- [ ] Can you build a five-color palette and explain the role each color plays in the interface?
- [ ] Can you explain why a wireframe deliberately excludes color and style?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Can you take one key screen and produce both its wireframe and its art-directed mockup, keeping the two concerns separate?
- [ ] Can you critique a design where the UX is clear but the art direction is inconsistent — and name the specific inconsistencies?
- [ ] Can you plan when in a project user testing should happen, and what each round should test?
