# Design Communication
*<font color="#8b949e">Critique the design, never the designer</font>*

</div>

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The rule</font>

Good design communication keeps the **subject of the sentence** on the design feature or mechanism. It can bring in personal feeling and emotion, but it should *never* include the designer. If you include your feelings as part of the feedback, you must also include *why* that feeling is happening.

| Sentence starts with... | Result |
|---|---|
| The feature, behavior, or mechanism | Productive — the idea gets better |
| The designer ("you...") | Personal — someone gets defensive |
| A judgment with no reason ("this is dumb") | Destructive — no one learns anything |

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Good design communication</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Positive</font>

- *"This mechanic can also be used in the following way..."*
- *"Another reason for this feature is that it can also work as a multiplayer mechanic, allowing for..."*

### <font color="#79c0ff">Negative (critical, but still good)</font>

- *"The design doesn't cover how this mechanism will behave in this scenario..."*
- *"If this behavior or style were used, it might be confused with this other mechanism or gameplay mechanic."*
- *"It's not clear to me how a user should be able to do this — I'm confused about the objective and the means."*

Notice that even the critical examples give the designer something concrete to fix.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Bad design communication</font>

Bad communication brings the designer into the conversation, or passes judgment without reasoning through the emotion behind it.

| Bad example | Why it fails |
|---|---|
| *"This is dumb. I don't like it."* | No explanation. Not useful. Destructive to progress |
| *"You should not have made the character jump that high."* | The subject of the sentence is the **designer**, not the design. This is how feedback turns into personal attack — and how people start taking critique personally |

The fix for the second example is one word of surgery: *"The jump height makes the platforming section trivial, because the player can skip every obstacle."* Same critique — now it is about the design, it explains why, and it can be acted on.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Quick self-check before you speak</font>

1. Is the subject of my sentence a feature or mechanism — not a person?
2. If I expressed a feeling, did I explain *why* I feel it?
3. Did I give the designer something they can actually act on?
