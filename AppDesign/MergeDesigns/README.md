# Merging Designs
*<font color="#8b949e">One class, one design — combined pairwise, like merge sort with opinions</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Reinforce</font>

</div>

---

Today the class converges. We choose a **single design as a class**, then work as a class to mature it — and then distill everyone's contributions into one comprehensive design. The distillation happens in stages and functionally behaves much like **merge sort** (apart from the Big O): pairs combine their work, then merged pairs combine with other merged pairs, until one design remains.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Part 1 — Build out the chosen design</font>

Add yourself to the chosen project as a **collaborator** on GitHub.

> **Warning:**
> **Clone the repository — do NOT fork.** The whole class works inside one shared repository from here on. Forks fragment the class's work; branches keep it together.

Then add the following to the chosen design:

- Create a **branch** for your design work
- Add **2 meaningful features**
- Add **2 less important modifications** to the design

---

## <font color="#388bfd">Part 2 — Merge pairwise</font>

Group up with a random classmate (chosen at random), read through each other's additions, and combine your designs into the most meaningful, best version. Communicate using this exact protocol:

### <font color="#79c0ff">The P1/P2 conversation protocol</font>

1. **P1:** Explain each major idea
2. **P2:** Respond with one reason why this idea might be good **that the designer may not have thought about**
   - Explain a use case where the functionality appeals to an even broader audience
   - *Surfing game example: if the feature is character customization, additional appeal may include — choosing more difficult surfboards increases your score; choosing larger surfboards decreases it but makes the game easier to play*
3. **P2:** Respond with one critical issue the designer may have overlooked, in terms of:
   - User experience
   - One of the brainstormed ideas about what is *not* enjoyable in a game
4. **Switch P1 and P2** and repeat for all major ideas
5. **For minor ideas:** give quick first impressions, both positive and critical, *of the idea* — keep the critique of the idea central to the conversation

### <font color="#79c0ff">Then merge in Git</font>

- Create a **branch for your joint work**
- **Merge your two branches into it**, keeping only your revised ideas
- **Push** the new branch

> **Note:**
> Be prepared to repeat this exercise in greater detail. When larger groups merge, you will be expected to mount a more robust defense and reasoning of your design.

## <font color="#388bfd">How to communicate design</font>

Good communication keeps the **subject of the sentence** on the design feature or mechanism. It can bring in personal feeling and emotion, but should *never* include the designer. If you include your feelings as feedback, you must include *why* the feeling is happening.

**Good — positive:**

- *"This mechanic can also be used in the following way..."*
- *"Another reason for this feature is that it can also be used as a multiplayer mechanic to allow for..."*

**Good — negative:**

- *"The design doesn't cover how this mechanism will behave in this scenario..."*
- *"If this behavior or style was used, it may be confused with this other mechanism or gameplay mechanic."*
- *"It's not clear to me how a user should be able to do this. I'm confused as to the objective or means."*

**Bad:**

- *"This is dumb. I don't like it."* — no explanation, not useful, destructive to progress
- *"You should not have made the character jump that high."* — the subject is the **designer**, not the design; this is how feedback becomes personal attack

The full phrasebook lives in [Design Communication](../Docs/design-communication.md) — keep it open during the exercise.

## <font color="#388bfd">Skill Building</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Can you explain why the class clones the chosen repository instead of forking it?
- [ ] Can you recite the P1/P2 protocol steps for a major idea?
- [ ] Can you state what your individual branch must contain (2 meaningful features, 2 lesser modifications)?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Can you respond to a partner's idea with a benefit the designer did not think of?
- [ ] Can you raise a critical issue phrased so the subject is the design, not the designer?
- [ ] Can you merge two branches into a joint branch that keeps only the revised ideas?

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Can you explain how the pairwise merging process resembles merge sort — and where the analogy breaks?
- [ ] Can you defend one of your features against a valid critique without dismissing the critique?
- [ ] Can you decide, in a merge, which of two overlapping features to drop — and justify it purely on user value?
