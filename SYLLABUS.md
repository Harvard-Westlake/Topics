## Course Overview

This course focuses on the most practical topics in computer science — building AI from the ground up, adapting to new technologies, collaborating on shared code, and leveraging AI as a programming tool. Students explore a range of modern topics including Java, JavaScript, Web2/3, AI model building, Git, Assembly, Python, and Big Data / parallel programming, culminating in the creation of a tech-industry-style app or product.

## Learning Objectives

- Develop proficiency in reading and contributing to others' code
- Master the use of AI tools for problem-solving and learning
- Gain experience in a variety of programming languages and paradigms
- Understand and apply modern software development practices
- Enhance technical communication and collaboration skills
- Cultivate critical thinking and ethical consideration in technology use

## Required Materials

- A **charged laptop** and its **charger** — every class meeting

> <font color="#e3b341">**NOTE**</font> — Students without a working computer at the start of class will be marked tardy.

## Course Topics

| Topic | Focus |
|---|---|
| Git Version Control | Repositories, commits, branching, and collaborating through forks and reviews |
| Unit Testing | Writing tests that specify behavior and catch regressions early |
| Web Development | JavaScript, hosting, persistence, and building interactive web widgets |
| UX / Product Development Cycle | Design, critique, iteration, and technical specification |
| Big Data Analysis / Parallel Programming | Working with large datasets and splitting computation across processes |
| Class Capstone App | A tech-industry-style app designed, built, and shipped by teams |
| Blockchain / Web3 | Hashing, wallets, smart contracts, and decentralized applications |
| AI Modeling and Implementation | Building a working GPT-style language model from the ground up |

## Success Strategies

- Balance self-reliance with proactive help-seeking.
- Use AI tools effectively for problem-solving and learning.
- Collaborate with classmates, especially on shared assignments.
- When asking for help, be prepared to explain:
  1. your AI-assisted research results,
  2. your goal, and
  3. the specific error or problem you are encountering.

## AI Integration

- AI tools are a primary resource for learning and problem-solving in this course.
- Learn to craft effective prompts and critically evaluate AI-generated responses.
- Understand the limitations and potential biases of AI tools.

> <font color="#f85149">**WARNING**</font> — **For assignments labeled "non-AI," use of AI tools is prohibited and is treated as an Honor Code violation.**

## Collaborative Learning

- For shared assignments (e.g., environment setup), the class receives a collective grade.
- Students are encouraged to help each other — it benefits both individual and class scores.
- Develop skills in explaining concepts and troubleshooting others' issues.

## Catching Up

If you fall behind, don't hesitate to seek help from instructors or classmates. Communicate your challenges promptly, and we'll work together to bridge any gaps in understanding.

## Technology Use Policy

- Use laptops responsibly; minimize distractions.
- Follow instructions to maintain focus on lesson objectives.

## Academic Integrity

- Complete assigned code individually unless explicitly instructed otherwise.
- Collaboration through discussion and peer assistance is encouraged on shared assignments.
- Always cite AI-generated content or solutions.
- When in doubt about the appropriateness of assistance, consult the teacher.

## Grading

### Grade Composition

| Component | Weight |
|---|---|
| Projects (Including Project Assessments) | 80% |
| Quizzes | 15% |
| Class Participation | 5% |

### Assignment Categories and Late Penalties

| Category | Typical use | Late penalty | Days counted |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Standard** | The most common category, especially in second semester | `score × 0.95 ^ days_late` — Harvard-Westlake's standard late policy: 5% off per class day late | Weekends and holidays excluded |
| **Time-Critical** | Important work, often during app development | `score × 0.89 ^ class_periods_late` — 11% off per class period late; also applies if absent for a sports commitment, or not turned in by the end of the day it's due | Class periods counted; weekends and holidays excluded |
| **Essential** | Core learning objectives, often collaborative | `score × 0.875 ^ days_late` | Weekends count; holidays do not |

> <font color="#e3b341">**NOTE**</font> — Late penalties are deliberately structured to introduce a concept you will meet again in reinforcement learning: discounted rewards.
