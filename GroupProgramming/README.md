# Group Programming
*<font color="#8b949e">From solo coder to engineering team — issues, branches, reviews, and handoffs</font>*

<font color="#a371f7">Reinforce</font>

</div>

---

This module is the moment you stop being a solo coder and become part of an engineering team. Everything changes when other people depend on your work: what you build matters less than whether your teammates can find it, trust it, and continue it. From here on, every piece of work is **tracked in an issue**, **shipped in a branch**, **reviewed by someone who didn't write it**, **integrated into a protected `main`**, **demonstrated from `main`**, and finally **handed off with documentation good enough that a stranger can pick the project up and keep going**.

You'll build on the design document and technical specification your team produced in [App Design and Planning](../AppDesign/), and you'll use every Git skill from [Git Usage](../GitUsage/) — cloning, branching, merging, forking, and pull requests — under real pressure, with real teammates waiting on you.

---

## <font color="#388bfd">The shape of the unit</font>

The lessons follow the life cycle of a real team project:

1. **Learn the work cycle** — issue, branch, review — and what it means to *deliver* something every single day, even when the work turns out to be harder than expected.
2. **Run sprints** — plan, commit to what you can actually finish, and ship it three classes in a row.
3. **Integrate an MVP** — merge `main` into your branch, test, and land a working end-to-end product on `main`.
4. **Prove it** — demo from `main` and defend your own merged code in front of the class.
5. **Communicate forward** — write issues so precise a stranger can implement them, document the project for handoff, then actually *be* that stranger in a classmate's repo.
6. **Ship it** — close every pull request, deploy, and submit a live, working product.

> **Note:**
> Before you begin, your whole team needs a clear, shared understanding of your project's layout, priorities, and build order. If you don't have that, go back to your [App Design and Planning](../AppDesign/) documents and prepare properly first.

## <font color="#388bfd">☑️ Check for Understanding</font>

### <font color="#79c0ff">Introductory</font>

- [ ] Name the three steps of the team work cycle (issue, branch, review) in order.
- [ ] Explain why work merged into `main` must be reviewed by someone who didn't write it.
- [ ] State what a deliverable is in one sentence.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Intermediate</font>

- [ ] Explain why communicating your work clearly can matter more than the amount of code you write.
- [ ] Describe what "demonstrated from `main`" means and why a feature-branch demo doesn't count.
- [ ] List what a handoff needs so a stranger can continue the project.

### <font color="#79c0ff">Advanced</font>

- [ ] Trace one feature through the full life cycle: issue → branch → pull request → review → protected `main` → demo → documentation.
- [ ] Explain how the tech spec from App Design and Planning drives the issues, branches, and reviews in this unit.
- [ ] Argue why a team that ships less code with full review discipline beats a team that ships more code without it.
